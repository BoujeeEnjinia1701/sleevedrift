# SleeveDrift user requirements

What the people who would use or stage the kit need, written from their side. They are drawn from the users and stakeholders in SVD-PRB-001 (Tables 1 and 2) and from the Silkyara reports cited there; they were used to score the concept options (SVD-OPT-001). The product requirements that meet them are in SVD-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix SVD-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). Nothing here is agreed with a co-design partner yet.

> **Safety:** SleeveDrift is an open engineering reference for equipment used by people in a rescue pipe under collapse debris. It is not certified rescue, mining or confined-space equipment.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Safety | Head protection at the face | The person digging at the face has solid cover over the head and shoulders so that a fall of debris does not reach them | High | Hand-mining rescue crews (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Spoil leaves the pipe | Spoil is carried out of the pipe at least as fast as the face produces it, so the dig is never waiting for the haul | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; portal crews |
| UR-003 | Usability | The haul is not heavy work | The portal crew can haul with ordinary effort, for a whole shift, without special strength | Medium | Portal crews |
| UR-004 | Safety | Fresh air at the face | Clean air reaches the digger all the time, in every part of the pipe | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; rescue commanders |
| UR-005 | Safety | Bad air is detected | The crew is warned at once of low oxygen, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulphide or flammable gas | High | Rescue commanders; confined-space practice |
| UR-006 | Performance | A casualty comes out fast | A person who is injured or trapped at the face can be brought out of the pipe within minutes | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; rescue commanders |
| UR-007 | Deployment and assembly | Fits the pipe that is there | Every piece goes in through the pipe and works inside it, with room left for a person to crawl past | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; tunnel contractors |
| UR-008 | Deployment and assembly | Ready within hours | The kit is installed and working within a couple of hours of arriving at the portal | Medium | Rescue commanders |
| UR-009 | Safety | Nothing powered in the pipe | No mains or engine-driven machine inside the pipe, where gas, dust and water collect and a fault cannot be reached | High | Rescue commanders; confined-space practice |
| UR-010 | Environment and durability | Lasts a rescue | The kit keeps working through days of continuous shifts in dust, grit and damp | Medium | Rescue commanders; portal crews |
| UR-011 | Logistics | Moves by road and by hand | The kit travels in cases one or two people can lift, and gets to the portal on foot if the road ends | Medium | Rescue commanders; tunnel contractors |
| UR-012 | Safety | A mistake cannot break the kit on a person | Nobody can overload the ropes and fittings by heaving too hard, and the kit stops before it hurts someone | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; portal crews |
| UR-013 | Cost | Affordable to hold in store | A contractor or a force can afford to hold a kit on standby, as part of emergency planning | Medium | Tunnel contractors; rescue commanders |
| UR-014 | Logistics | Buildable and mendable by a workshop | A general steel workshop can make the made parts from stock section and plate, and everything else can be bought | Medium | Tunnel contractors; local fabrication workshops |
| UR-015 | Usability | Face and portal can talk | The digger and the portal crew can signal each other at any moment, without batteries or radio | High | Hand-mining rescue crews; portal crews |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
